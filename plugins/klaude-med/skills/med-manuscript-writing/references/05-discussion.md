# Discussion — rules distilled from 14-paper corpus

Source tags: A1 and A3 = writing guides; B1 NEJM RCT; B2 BMJ pragmatic RCT (NS primary); B3 BMJ cohort; B4 BMJ population observational; C1 dose-response MA; C2 NMA; C3 pairwise MA of RCTs; D1 SR+MA; D2 SR without MA; D3 umbrella review. Word counts marked (est.) are estimates from per-paper notes.

## Job of this section
Say what the data allow, weigh it against other evidence, explain why it might be so, and state honestly how far it can be trusted and used. It is a weighing exercise, not a re-run of Results [A1]. Every claim must stay inside the design's reach [A3].

## Move structure by article type

**RCT [B1,B2]**
1. Principal finding in one sentence (design position + primary result; no new numbers beyond the headline) [B2]
2. Clinical meaning: judge the CI against a pre-specified or externally cited threshold, and offer an alternative threshold [B1,B2]
3. Secondary outcomes and safety, in one paragraph each; safety compared with earlier trials [B1,B2]
4. Gap in prior evidence and what the trial now adds; comparison with the closest earlier trial, with a stated reason for any difference (population, follow-up, adherence) [B2]
5. Explain the control-arm or surprising result via design features, not asserted causes [B2]
6. Strengths (short; may be carried by one "largest/most comprehensive" sentence) [B1,B2]
7. Limitations: ordinal list of concrete gaps, or a single main limitation answered by a sensitivity analysis [B1,B2]
8. Closing: bounded restatement; no extrapolation [B1,B2]

**Observational cohort / population study [B3,B4]**
1. Summary of main findings and robustness (no new numbers) [B3]; or context + principal finding, naming the data source [B4]
2. Comparison with other studies, organised by outcome site/subgroup; meta-analyses first, then single cohorts [B3]
3. Explanation of discrepant prior results by power, population, exposure definition [B3]
4. Mechanisms in order of evidence strength; test each against the data (adjustment, interaction) [B3,B4]
5. Null finding with power caveat [B3]; unexplained subgroup differences admitted plainly [B4]
6. Strengths, then limitations, each paired with direction of bias [B3,B4,A3]
7. Policy implications (own subheading when warranted) [B4]; conditional chain before any strong noun [B3]

**Meta-analysis**
- Pairwise [C3,D1]: dense opening (scope + headline) → last comprehensive review versus this one → secondary outcomes versus other reviews with a reason for differences → surrogate-to-clinical bridge with its design limit → mechanism/analogy with limits → criticism pre-empted by interaction test → strengths → limitations by GRADE domain, with reasons for not downgrading → absolute-risk or threshold translation → agenda naming a design or registered trial [C3]. Subgroup proxy limits and screening/definition changes over time belong here [D1].
- Dose-response [C1]: summary with robustness subgroups → comparison (agreement stated first; contradictions explained by exposure definition) → confounder-check result → mechanisms, opening with the admission that mechanisms are unclear → strengths (ordinal) → limitations with bias direction and generalisability.
- NMA [C2]: ranking/headline → class context and real-world use → each class carries its own caveat (imprecision, single driving study) → limitations of evidence (registry gaps, heterogeneity, certainty reasons, transitivity, hub reliance) → design restrictions justified → clinical/policy implications, including cost and access.

**Systematic review without MA [D2]**: restate focus + main finding, with evidence-quality concern immediately after → setting/geographic concentration → strongest result with alternative explanation → mixed findings labelled by design cause → policy context → related tools out of scope → research need. Pair with SWiM-style transparency about what synthesis could not support.

**Umbrella review [D3]**: scope + headline share → strongest evidence tier → grade/heterogeneity distribution and what a low grade means → mechanisms, one pathway per paragraph, flagged as hypothesised → strengths → design-level limitations (no confounder re-adjustment, overlap, measurement) → conflict with related reviews explained → policy by instrument → bounded conclusions.

## Template bank
**Principal finding**
1. In this [design] of [N] [participants/studies], [intervention/exposure] [did/did not] [change] [primary outcome] at [timepoint]; [headline estimate, CI].
2. [Study acronym] is the largest [design] to compare [A] with [B] in [population].
3. [Exposure] was associated with a [higher/lower] risk of [outcome] and [subtype]; the association held in [n] sensitivity analyses.

**Clinical meaning**
4. A change of this size [meets/falls short of] the [threshold] set by [authority/prespecified margin]; [alternative threshold] has also been proposed.
5. The [lower/upper] CI limit excludes the prespecified worthwhile difference of [X], so [intervention] did not show a worthwhile benefit.
6. In absolute terms, this corresponds to about [n] fewer events per [1000] over [period].

**Comparison with other studies**
7. Our estimate [agrees with/differs from] [prior study] ([n] participants), which [reported ...]; the difference may reflect [power / population / exposure definition].
8. [Prior review] pooled [exposure as food group], whereas we analysed [nutrient]; this could explain [discrepancy].
9. Until [this study], evidence on [management question] was limited to [design/setting].
10. Restricting to studies with [adjustment] left [association A] largely unchanged and weakened [association B], so [confounder] is unlikely to account for [A].

**Mechanisms**
11. The mechanisms by which [exposure] could affect [outcome] remain uncertain.
12. [Pathway] could contribute, since [intermediate] is a recognised risk factor for [outcome]; adjusting for it [attenuated/did not change] the estimate, consistent with [partial mediation/no mediation].
13. This pathway is hypothesised; [mediator] was not measured in [all models].

**Null / unexplained results**
14. We found no statistically significant association between [exposure] and [outcome], but the study could not exclude [effect size] because [few events/low exposure range].
15. The data do not explain why [subgroup] differs; we avoid speculation beyond [what the data support].

**Strengths**
16. Strengths include [design feature 1], [sample/coverage], and [measurement validation], which [reduce a named bias].

**Limitations (with direction)**
17. First, [limitation]; this would most probably [bias toward null / overestimate] the [estimate] because [reason].
18. [Variable] was measured by [method]; non-differential error would be expected to [attenuate] the association, while differential error could act in either direction.
19. Residual confounding by [factor] cannot be excluded; it is likely to [inflate/deflate] the estimate because [relation to exposure and outcome].
20. Loss to follow-up was [%]; a [sensitivity analysis] gave [similar/different] results, which [supports/limits] confidence in the primary analysis.
21. We did not downgrade for [GRADE domain] because [reason]; [alternative source of bias] would more likely understate the effect.
22. Our findings concern [average effects / population] and may not apply to [setting] where [characteristic] differs.

**Implications**
23. For [clinicians], these results [support/do not support] [action] in [population]; [other consideration, e.g. cost, tolerability] should enter shared decisions.
24. If replicated in [setting] and supported by [mechanistic evidence], [exposure] would be a candidate modifiable risk factor for [outcome].
25. [Policy option] could be considered, with attention to [equity/coverage] effects seen in [subgroup].

**Future research**
26. A [design] in [population] that [randomises/measures X at Y] would test [question] under weaker assumptions.
27. [Ongoing trial, registration ID] will address [uncertainty] in [population] over [duration].
28. Individual participant data would allow [subgroup/effect-modification] analysis that aggregate data cannot.

**Closing**
29. Among [population], [intervention] [changed measured outcomes]; effects on [unmeasured outcome] remain untested.
30. Replication in [setting] is needed before [broader claim].

## Tense / voice / person
| Content | Tense | Voice/person |
|---|---|---|
| Own results | Past | "we found"; active [B3,B4,D3] |
| Interpretation, established knowledge | Present | Active or passive [B1,C3] |
| Prior studies | Past ("reported") | Third person [B3] |
| Mechanisms | Present with modals (may/might/could) | Impersonal [C1,D3] |
| Limitations | Past for what was done, present for consequence | "we" or impersonal; avoid defensive tone [B2,C2] |
| Implications | Present/conditional | "would", "could", "should" for prescriptions [B3,B4] |
| Conclusion | Present perfect or present | Bounded to the comparison tested [B2] |

## Claim-strength ladder
Strongest to weakest; use the highest rung the design earns.
1. "showed / reduced" — RCT primary or key secondary with CI excluding null, own result only [B1].
2. "was associated with" — observational estimates; adjusted [B3,B4,C1]. Moves to "drives" only with a mediation analysis and a stated caveat [B3].
3. "suggests / is consistent with" — interpretation across studies [C3,B4].
4. "may / might / could" — mechanisms, extrapolation, explanations [C1,D1].
5. "cannot be excluded / remains unclear" — unresolved alternatives [D1,C2].
6. "no statistically significant difference" / "did not differ significantly" — NS results. Add CI and what the CI excludes. Use "not superior" for a failed superiority test [B2]. Never "no difference", "equally effective", "equivalent", "proves no effect" unless a non-inferiority/equivalence margin was pre-specified [A3,B3].
Rules:
- A CI crossing 1 is never "reduced by X%".
- Causal verbs only for randomised comparisons or with stated assumptions [A3]; the same verb can change meaning (A3).
- Strong policy language ("strongly support") exceeds an observational design; downgrade it [C1].
- Absence of evidence: say the study was not powered to exclude [effect size] [A3,B3].
- Do not read p between .05 and .10 as a "trend" [A3].

## Numbers & statistics formatting
- Restate only 2–3 headline numbers; full sets live in Results [B4,C2]. Give absolute and relative effect together when translating to practice [C3].
- Estimate then 95% CI in parentheses; match decimals to Results; state NS explicitly: "(95% CI −1.2 to 3.4)". Do not repeat p values when the CI conveys the point [B3,B4].
- Name denominators (n/N, %) for proportions, losses and adherence [B2].
- Pair magnitude with a practical threshold; significance alone is not importance [A3].
- Quote cross-study ranges as multiplicative ("1.2–1.5-fold") only when sourced [D1].
- Meta-analysis: I² with direction/cause of heterogeneity; GRADE certainty in words plus the domain that drove it ("moderate, owing to imprecision") [C3,C2]. Low certainty means the data are thin, not that there is no association [D3].
- Conflicts with national or external rates: one sentence with numbers and units (per 100 000 person-years) [B3].

## Reporting-guideline checklist
- **CONSORT 2010 items 20–22 (CONSORT 2025 renumbered; map separately)**: limitations (bias, imprecision, multiplicity, unplanned analyses); generalisability; interpretation consistent with results, balancing benefit and harm, in context of other evidence [B1,B2].
- **STROBE items 18–21**: key results restated; limitations with sources and direction of bias; cautious overall interpretation; generalisability [B3,B4].
- **PRISMA 2020 items 23a–d**: interpretation in context of other evidence; limitations of included evidence; limitations of the review process; implications for practice, policy, future research [C3,D1,D2].
- **PRISMA-NMA / GRADE (CINeMA)**: certainty and reasons; transitivity, imprecision, indirect-evidence reliance [C2].
- **MOOSE**: bias discussion for observational MA, generalisability, alternative explanations [C1].
- **SWiM**: limits of narrative synthesis; heterogeneity unexamined [D2].
- Always check: no new results introduced; conclusion matches the Abstract.

## Do / Don't
- Do open with what the design and data allow without doubt [A3,B2]. / Don't open with background or a "this study fills a gap" boast.
- Do state limitation + direction of bias + effect on the estimate [B3,A3]. / Don't list generic limitations ("small sample", "observational") with no consequence.
- Do argue a confounder would bias conservatively only if directionally sound [B3]. / Don't assume measurement error always attenuates [A3].
- Do explain discrepancies with prior work by specific differences (exposure definition, population, power) [C1,B2]. / Don't dismiss earlier studies or cite only agreeing ones.
- Do cover selection, measurement and confounding bias [A3]. / Don't discuss only confounding.
- Do put uncertainty in the first sentence of a mechanism paragraph [C1]. / Don't stack unreferenced mechanisms.
- Do end with a concrete design (population, comparison, endpoint, trial ID) [A3,C3]. / Don't write "more research is needed" alone.
- Do pre-empt a design criticism with a tested analysis [C3]. / Don't claim a subgroup or post hoc pattern as confirmed [A3].
- Do keep a mixed-result paragraph neutral ("mixed") [D2]. / Don't use "disappointing", "promising", "striking", "trend towards".
- Do keep claims within the measured outcomes [B1]. / Don't say "first study" unless a systematic search shows it; prefer "largest" with a stated criterion [B2,D2].

## LLM-draft failure modes → fix
| Tell | Why it reads as AI / is wrong | Rewrite pattern |
|---|---|---|
| "These findings underscore the importance of…" / "pave the way" | Empty emphasis; no content | State the specific action or threshold the finding supports |
| First paragraph restates every Results number | Duplicates Results; reader skips | One headline number, then robustness in one clause |
| "Our results suggest [X] is effective" for NS result | Equates NS with equivalence or effect | "did not differ significantly (CI …); the CI excludes/does not exclude [margin]" |
| "novel", "first study to…", "fills a critical gap" | Unverifiable; hype | "largest [design] to date in [population]", only if checked |
| Limitations: "small sample, retrospective, generalisability" | No direction, no consequence | Name limit, bias direction, how it moves the estimate, any mitigating analysis |
| Mechanism chain with five citations in one sentence, asserted as fact | Speculation presented as established | One pathway per paragraph, modal verbs, tested if possible |
| "Further research is needed" closing | Contains no information | Specify design, population, endpoint, and the assumption it relaxes |
| Triplet lists ("robust, reliable, and rigorous") | Rhythmic filler | Replace with the checkable design feature |
| Causal verbs ("drives", "leads to") in observational work | Overclaims | "was associated with"; state the assumptions a causal reading needs |
| Concluding paragraph that summarises the whole paper again | Redundant | Bounded takeaway in two clauses, plus the main caveat |
| Uniform paragraph length and lock-step "Firstly… Secondly…" in every paragraph | Template feel | Ordinals only in the limitations list; vary sentence length |
| Unsourced reassurance ("bias is negligible", "we believe our sample is representative") | Unsupported belief | Cite validation data or soften to "likely small because [reason]" |

## Mini worked example (fictional)
**Before (LLM-style):**
"This groundbreaking study provides crucial insights into the pivotal role of drug X in heart failure. Our results demonstrate that drug X is as effective as placebo for readmission, underscoring its safety. Notably, drug X significantly improved quality of life, highlighting its transformative potential. Limitations include the small sample and single-centre design. Further research is needed to explore these findings in more diverse populations."

**After (journal-style):**
"In this trial of 412 adults with heart failure, drug X did not significantly reduce 90 day readmission (22.8% v 26.5%; risk difference −3.7 percentage points, 95% CI −11.4 to 4.0). The interval does not exclude a reduction of 11 percentage points, and the trial was powered to detect only a 9 point difference; the result is therefore inconclusive, not evidence of no effect. Quality of life scores were higher with drug X (mean difference 4.1 points, 95% CI 0.8 to 7.4), a difference near the 5 point threshold proposed for clinical importance. This was a secondary outcome and was not adjusted for multiplicity. Recruitment at one centre may limit generalisability, and the 14% loss to follow-up, higher in the placebo arm, could have biased the readmission estimate toward drug X; a multiple imputation analysis gave similar results. A multicentre trial powered for a 6 point reduction would resolve the question."
