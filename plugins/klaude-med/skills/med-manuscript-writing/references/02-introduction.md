# Introduction / Background — rules distilled from 14-paper corpus

Source tags: A1-A4 writing guides; B1 NEJM RCT, B2 BMJ pragmatic RCT, B3 BMJ cohort, B4 BMJ population observational; C1 dose-response MA, C2 network MA, C3 pairwise MA; D1 SR+MA, D2 SR without MA, D3 umbrella review.

## Job of this section
Make the reader understand why this question, in this population, with this design, needs an answer now. It does so by argument (burden, known, unknown, consequence), not by adjectives. It ends on the aim, and it never contains results or conclusions [A1,A2,A4].

## Move structure by article type

Universal funnel [A1,A2]: (1) burden/context, (2) what is known, (3) what is unknown (the gap), (4) why the gap matters, (5) aim/hypothesis as the final sentence of the final paragraph. Every paragraph orients, states the known, and lands on the next unknown [A1 R6]. Each paragraph carries one idea, signalled by its first sentence [A2].

### RCT [B1,B2]
| Para | Move |
|---|---|
| 1 | Define the condition and its mechanism/consequences; give prevalence or incidence with a population-based number, split by sex/age if relevant [B1,B2] |
| 2 | Current standard care, one stated limit per option (gap built by contrast, not by adjectives); end with "no approved/proven option" or "evidence comparing options is scarce" [B1,B2] |
| 3 | Rationale for the intervention in hops: disease link, guideline gap, pharmacology or mechanism, hedged ("may") [B1]; or patient/system cost framed as a conditional [B2] |
| 4 | Prior evidence on the intervention (one or two sentences), then the objective sentence: "we report..." (NEJM) [B1] or "the primary objective was to compare A and B in [population]" (BMJ) [B2] |

### Observational cohort / population study [B3,B4]
| Para | Move |
|---|---|
| 1 | Exposure trend or programme scale, with a global/local burden figure and source [B3,B4] |
| 2 | Established effects of the exposure on intermediate or other outcomes; the substitute/alternative and its own harms, or local programme history (natural experiment) [B3,B4] |
| 3 | Link to the study outcome: plausible pathways listed with "may"; prior findings grouped by direction, not study by study; equity or subgroup concern as a conditional chain ("if X, this could Y") [B3,B4] |
| 4 | Gap in specific terms (few prospective studies, inconsistent site-specific results, exposures rarely analysed separately) then an aim sentence naming exposure, outcome and design [B3,B4] |

### Meta-analysis
- Pairwise MA of RCTs [C3]: (1) define the exposure with its metric and cut-offs; (2) place it in guidelines and standard management; (3) prior reviews and cohorts, then the gap as three or so concrete deficits (outdated guideline, dated review, new trials since the last search); (4) aim, who commissioned it if applicable, and the certainty framework (GRADE).
- Network MA [C2]: (1) burden in cited steps (definition, individual burden, societal burden); (2) treatment landscape by guideline tier, closing on disagreement about ranking; (3) why a network approach is needed (many options, earlier syntheses covered only a subset), then one aim sentence containing population, comparators and design.
- Dose-response MA of cohorts [C1]: (1) burden of the outcomes; (2) exposure trend; (3) mechanistic/biomarker evidence including a counter-finding; (4) conflicting cohort findings clustered by direction; (5) gap stated as missing shape/strength of the dose-response, then the aim.
- Justifying a new review given prior reviews [C3,D1,D3]: name what earlier work did not do, as a checkable, scoped deficit: unadjusted estimates, outcomes not reported, dated search, new trials since, limited generalisability, narrow scope [C2,C3,D1,D2]. Frame as incomplete coverage, never as flaws of earlier authors [C2].

### Systematic review without MA [D2]
Introduction: field and construct (with a named measure), then the mechanism linking exposure to outcomes with one or two attributed numbers, then the policy/practice stake ("therefore..."). Background: survey of approaches (cite reviews), absence of consensus, jurisdictional contrast with one worked example of a method's failure, then scope gap of earlier reviews. Aim goes in a labelled paragraph at the start of the Review section, or last in Background. Reason for no MA is given in Methods, not here.

### Umbrella review [D3]
(1) Define the exposure with its classification system and its share of the food/health system as ranges; (2) trend and drivers; (3) proposed mechanisms (hedged) and the stakes; (4) several primary reviews exist but no comprehensive overview of the whole evidence base; (5) aim tied to a live policy debate.

## Length budget (words; estimates from corpus, no journal limits verified)
| Type | Words | Paragraphs |
|---|---|---|
| RCT | 380-450 [B1,B2] | 4-6 |
| Cohort / population | 420-480 [B3,B4] | 4 |
| Pairwise / dose-response MA | 380-400 [C1,C3] | 4 |
| NMA | ~330 [C2] | 3 |
| SR+MA | ~290 [D1] | 3 |
| SR without MA | Intro ~480 plus Background ~520 [D2] | 3 + 4 |
| Umbrella | ~550 [D3] | 3 (dense) |
- Guide advice: about one double-spaced page, 250-300 words as default, 2-4 paragraphs for a hypothesis-testing paper; a long introduction suggests thin data [A2]. Target 300-450 words unless the journal template says otherwise.
- Paragraph 75-150 words, 3-4 sentences; sentence 25-30 words or fewer [A2].
- Citations per paragraph: not quantified in the corpus. Working estimate: 2-5 per paragraph, one to two sources per factual claim; clusters of 3+ only for a direction-grouped body of evidence [B3,C1]; no citation for textbook facts [A2].

## Template bank
All slots are generic. Fill from the user's data; do not invent numbers.

Burden and context
1. [Condition] is characterised by [mechanism], leading to [consequence 1] and [consequence 2] [ref].
2. About [N] [people/cases] are affected worldwide each year, and roughly [X]% have [severe form] [ref].
3. In [setting], [outcome] occurs at [rate] per [denominator] [person-years], highest in [subgroup] aged [range] [ref].
4. [Exposure] has increased [by X%] between [year] and [year], and is now linked to about [N] [events] annually [ref].
5. The condition accounts for [burden measure] and [cost/resource figure] in [setting] [ref].

What is known
6. [Standard therapy] improves [measure], but its effect is limited by [limit] [ref].
7. [Guideline body] recommends [intervention] for [population], although [specific disagreement/uncertainty] persists [ref].
8. Observational studies link [exposure] with [intermediate outcome 1] and [outcome 2] [refs].
9. [Exposure] could act on [outcome] through [pathway 1], [pathway 2] or [pathway 3] [ref].
10. Earlier cohorts have reported [direction A] [refs], [no significant association] [refs] and [direction B] [refs].

Gap
11. However, evidence on [specific question] is [scarce/inconsistent/limited to short follow-up] [ref].
12. [Prior review] pooled [evidence type] but did not adjust for [confounder] or report [outcome], leaving [question] uncertain [ref].
13. The most recent comprehensive review searched to [year], and [n] trials have appeared since [ref].
14. No review has yet examined [shape/subgroup/comparator set] across [evidence base].
15. Existing syntheses cover only [subset of agents/outcomes/settings], so the relative ranking of [options] is unclear.
16. Although [prior claim], [limitation of that work]; whether [unknown] holds in [population] has not been tested.

Why it matters
17. Without [information], [decision-maker] cannot choose between [option A] and [option B] for [patients].
18. If uptake of [intervention] is lower among [group at greatest risk], this could widen differences in [outcome].
19. [Guideline/policy body] is revising [document], so a current appraisal of the evidence is needed.

Aim / hypothesis (final sentence)
20. We therefore examined whether [exposure] is associated with [outcome] in [population], using [design].
21. The primary objective was to compare [intervention A] with [intervention B] on [primary outcome] in [population].
22. Here we report [trial/programme] assessing the efficacy and safety of [intervention] in adults with [condition]. (NEJM style [B1])
23. We conducted a systematic review and [meta-analysis/network meta-analysis] of [study designs] to [quantify/compare/summarise] [association or effect] of [exposure/interventions] on [outcomes] in [population].
24. To inform [guideline/policy update], [group] commissioned this review to [aim] and to rate the certainty of evidence using GRADE.
25. We hypothesised that [intervention] would [direction] [primary outcome] compared with [comparator]. (Only if the hypothesis was pre-specified.)
26. Secondary aims were to [subgroup/dose-response/equity analysis].

Signposts (use sparingly)
27. In contrast, [finding] ... / Despite this, ... / Taken together, ...
28. Building on [prior study], we [extension] because [limitation] remains unresolved.

## Tense, voice, person
| Element | Tense / voice | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Established facts, burden | Present | [A1,A2,B1-B4,C2,D1-D3] |
| Trends over time | Present perfect | "has increased", "has been introduced" [A2,B3,B4] |
| Findings of prior studies | Present perfect for bodies of work; past for a single dated study | The guide prefers present/present perfect; corpus uses past for individual studies [A2 vs B4,D1,C3]. Choose present perfect for groups, past for one dated study. |
| Gap | Present or present perfect | "is scarce", "has not been examined" |
| Mechanism | Present with modals (may, could) | [B1,C1,D3] |
| Aim | Past ("The aim was") for trials/cohorts; present ("we report", "we aimed") for NEJM-style | Either consistent with Methods [B1,B2,B3] |
| Person | "We" active for aim; no "I"; no "you" | [B3,B4,D1,C1] |
| Voice | Active default | Passive acceptable for "were included" |

## Claim-strength ladder (Introduction)
Strongest to weakest, and when allowed:
1. "is" / "are" (unhedged): only for well-established, cited, multi-source facts (epidemiology, guideline positions) [B1,B2].
2. "convincingly associated", "consistently linked": multiple cohorts plus meta-analysis support [B3].
3. "has been associated with", "have shown", "reported": single or few studies; match the verb to design (RCT: "reduced/did not significantly reduce"; observational: "associated with") [B3,C3].
4. "suggests", "emerging evidence", "may", "could", "might": mechanisms, biological plausibility, unproven rationale [B1,C1,D3].
5. "inconsistent", "controversial", "limited", "scarce": gap framing. Prefer "less investigated" and "rarely analysed" to "no data exist" [B3,C1].
- Absolute gap claims ("no study has...") only when scoped to an attribute ("no comprehensive overview", "no adjusted estimate") and verified by search; never "first" [D1,D3,C2].
- Causal language: only for RCT-backed prior findings. For cohort and cross-sectional prior work use association wording [C3,B4].
- Prior trial with non-significant primary result: write "did not significantly reduce" or "failed to show a significant benefit"; never "had no effect", "no difference", or "equally effective" unless a non-inferiority/equivalence margin was pre-specified.
- Never state or imply that the study's intervention works (no results, no "promising", no "novel") [B1,B2,A2].
- A prior CI crossing 1 is not "reduced risk by X%".

## Numbers and statistics formatting (Introduction)
- Few numbers: burden, trend, scale, and attributed prior-study figures only. No effect estimates or CIs from the present study [A1,A2,B3].
- Give year, source and denominator: "per 100 000 person-years", "in [year]" [B2,C1].
- Ranges for variable shares ("from x% in A to y% in B") [D3]; "more than N million" for round counts [B1,C2].
- Percentages and prior-study effects attributed to the cited study, with unit increments spelled out ("one more patient per nurse") [D2]. If quoting a prior HR/OR with CI crossing 1, label it non-significant.
- Dates of last review/guideline update stated explicitly when they carry the gap argument [C3].
- Superscript or bracket numbered citations; keep the same style as the journal [B1,A1].
- Define the metric and cut-offs in the text rather than relying on a glossary [C3].

## Reporting-guideline checklist
- [ ] CONSORT 2010 item 2a: scientific background and rationale; 2b: specific objectives or hypotheses (final sentence) [B1,B2].
- [ ] CONSORT 2025: background/rationale and specific objectives are still required; check the journal's current version (not verified from corpus).
- [ ] STROBE item 2 (background/rationale) and item 3 (objectives, including pre-specified hypotheses) [B3,B4]. (B4 note numbers them differently; follow the official checklist.)
- [ ] PRISMA 2020 item 3 (rationale in context of existing knowledge) and item 4 (objectives, with PICO elements) [C3,D1]. PRISMA-NMA same, plus rationale for network approach [C2]. MOOSE: problem definition and hypothesis statement [C1].
- [ ] SWiM: not an Introduction item; state grouping/synthesis plan in Methods [D2].
- [ ] Umbrella review: rationale must state what primary reviews exist and why overview is needed [D3].
- [ ] Objectives in the Introduction match the Abstract and Methods wording (outcome names repeated verbatim) [C2,D2].
- [ ] Prior reviews/trials cited fairly, including those that contradict the hypothesis [A2].

## Do / Don't
- Do open with a quantified, specific burden or context sentence. / Don't open with "X is an important issue" or a history of the field [A2,B1].
- Do build the gap by naming one limit per existing option or prior study. / Don't write "limited evidence" with no scope [B1,C3].
- Do end with a single aim sentence listing population, exposure/intervention, comparator, outcome, design. / Don't end with a results preview or conclusion [A2,C2].
- Do group prior findings by direction. / Don't list studies one by one [C1,B4].
- Do cite only papers you have read, pertinent and recent. / Don't cite textbook facts or reuse Discussion citations [A2].
- Do state why a new review is needed in checkable terms (date, scope, adjustment). / Don't claim "first" or "novel" [C3,D1].
- Do keep rationale conditional and hedged. / Don't assert benefit or harm before Results [B1,B4,D3].
- Do keep self-citation proportionate. / Don't lean on the authors' earlier work to prop up the rationale [A4].
- Do keep paragraphs to one idea. / Don't add a long literature review [A1,A2].
- Do match length to the journal and to the data. / Don't pad to compensate for a small study [A2].

## LLM-draft failure modes and fixes
| Tell | Why it reads as AI / is wrong | Rewrite pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Opens "X represents a pivotal challenge in the evolving landscape of..." | Stock vocabulary, no information | Start with a quantified burden: "[Condition] affects [N] people and accounts for [measure] [ref]." |
| Generic triplets ("prevalence, morbidity, and mortality") with no numbers | Padding, nothing checkable | Pick the one most relevant metric and give it a number, year and source |
| "Little is known about..." / "remains poorly understood" | Vague gap, hard to defend | Name the specific missing element: shape, subgroup, adjusted estimate, search date |
| "This novel study is the first to..." | Priority claims are usually false and are flagged by reviewers | State the gap and aim; if novelty is real, state what was not done before in scoped terms |
| Intro previews results or says "we found that..." | Removes the reason to read on [A2] | Stop at the aim; results go in Results |
| "Crucial", "vital", "paramount", "groundbreaking" | Adjectives replace argument | Show importance with consequence: cost, mortality, guideline conflict |
| Citation dumps of 8+ refs after one sentence or reference lists with no claim attached | Over-citation buries the aim [A2] | Cite 1-3 most pertinent; cluster only for direction-grouped bodies |
| Fabricated or imprecise numbers and references | Unverifiable claims | Insert slots; flag for the author to fill with checked sources |
| Rhetorical questions ("But what if...?") | Not journal register | Replace with a declarative gap sentence |
| Prior non-significant trial described as "showed no effect" | Absence of significance is not evidence of no effect | "did not show a statistically significant effect on [outcome]" |
| Mechanism stated as fact in the rationale | Overclaims before data | "may act through [pathway]" |
| Every paragraph opens with "Furthermore/Moreover/Additionally" | Mechanical flow, no logic | Use contrast and consequence markers (however, despite this, therefore) only where the logic turns |
| Equal-length paragraphs, all with a three-part list | Template feel | Vary lengths; let the gap paragraph be shorter than the context paragraph |

## Mini worked example (fictional)
Before (LLM-style):
"Heart failure represents a pivotal challenge in the modern healthcare landscape, with profound implications for patients, families, and health systems worldwide. Numerous therapies have been explored, yet much remains unknown about optimal management. Emerging evidence suggests that Drug X is a novel and promising agent that could transform outcomes. This groundbreaking study is the first to comprehensively investigate Drug X and reveals that it substantially improves survival."

After (journal-style):
"Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction affects about 32 million people worldwide, and 1-year hospital readmission exceeds 30% after an admission [ref]. Guidelines recommend diuretics for congestion and sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors for risk reduction, but diuretic resistance develops in a substantial minority of patients, and no therapy has been shown to reduce readmission in this group [ref]. Drug X, an oral aldosterone-pathway modulator, lowered natriuretic peptide levels in two small phase 2 trials (n=48 and n=71) [ref]; neither was powered for clinical outcomes. We therefore tested whether Drug X, added to standard care, reduces the first heart-failure hospitalisation within 12 months in adults with preserved ejection fraction, in a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial."
